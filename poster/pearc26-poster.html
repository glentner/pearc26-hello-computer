A strange game

Untrusted, yet a national imperative

In WarGames (1983), a computer concludes some games can only be survived, not won: the only winning move is not to play. Research science is tempted by that same fatalism about AI — decline to engage, wait for the norms to settle, keep the machine outside the lab door. But the board is already set:

  • AI is distrusted — for good reason. Peer review assumed a human behind every sentence; reproducibility assumes a documented method. An agent that will cheerfully write a Slurm script for a cluster you don't operate is not a colleague you trust unsupervised.
  • AI is also a mandate. MCP moved to the Linux Foundation; the U.S. Genesis Mission charged the national labs with uniting supercomputers and AI; serious voices call HPC "at a crossroads." The institutions that distrust the machine are told to build on it.

You don't resolve untrusted-yet-imperative by picking a side. You resolve it the way you resolve any hard experiment: make the method explicit, the evidence inspectable, and the failures cheap to catch. This paper was written to be that evidence.

Proactive engagement,
not prohibition.
The stakes — to the people who set the rules

If you fund research, edit a journal, chair a program committee, or run a lab: the question is no longer whether researchers use these tools, but whether the process is disclosed and reproducible when they do.

This factory is one concrete answer — an auditable, honest, agent-first pipeline whose every artifact is committed in the open. A proposal to stress-test, adopt, or better. The winning move is to write the rules of play while the game is young.

Three pillars at RCAC

What we actually do

On Gautschi today — and evolving.

1 Shared context

System-wide agent context deployed to /etc/agents.d — Markdown that describes the cluster to the agent, concatenated into AGENTS.md and symlinked per harness. 5 modules × 5 harnesses (Claude Code · Codex · Gemini · opencode · Warp), each shaped fact → command → "do not…" → why.

# slurm.md — written to the agent every job needs -A (account) and -p (partition) do NOT invent module names or partitions

2 MCP servers

Three open-source servers so an agent has "context that knows our clusters": rcac-mcp (~21 tools, local-first over your existing SSH), globus-mcp (transfers + compute), and a hosted, no-auth rcac-docs-mcp.

"If you can SSH to the cluster, your agent can too — and only with the access you already hold." No new service, no new credential.

3 Docs & training

A new Agentic AI section (11 pages) + a Gautschi "Using AI Agents" chapter — engage honestly rather than pretend the tools don't exist: research vs. operations, verify not trust (augment expertise, don't outsource it), and the blast-radius of destructive commands, allocation burn, and secret leakage.

"These tools are most dangerous precisely where you know the least. Ask why, not just what."

The rules — there are no cheat codes

You may not ship over the page budget, with a dangling \cite, an unsupported claim, an outline-drifted manuscript, or an undisclosed use of AI. Scope is cuttable; quality is not — a disinterested checker enforces it. That is the trust contract.
